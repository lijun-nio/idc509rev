             CBOR Encoded Certificate Revocation Management
                 draft-liao-cose-c509-revocation-latest

Abstract

   This document specifies CBOR-encoded PKI structures for use with C509
   certificates (draft-ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert), X.509 certificates
   (RFC 5280), and future certificate types.  It defines C509 CRL and
   C509 OCSP, compact CBOR encodings of X.509 Certificate Revocation
   Lists (RFC 5280) and OCSP messages (RFC 6960), respectively.  The
   structures defined in this document are certificate-type agnostic and
   can be used with C509 certificates, X.509 certificates, or future
   certificate types without modification.  C509 OCSP improves on RFC
   6960 by signing a wider set of fields to prevent algorithm-
   substitution and certificate-chain substitution attacks, replacing
   the two-hash issuer identity with a single certificate hash, and
   identifying all participants (requestor, responder, issuer) by a
   uniform certificate hash rather than type-specific fields.  C509 CRL
   and C509 OCSP are not wire-format-compatible with their DER-encoded
   X.509 counterparts and cannot be converted to or from them without
   semantic interpretation.  Additionally, this document specifies the
   PEM representation of the C509 structures following the framework of
   RFC 7468.

1.  Introduction

   Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) for constrained environments
   [RFC7228] requires compact certificate representations and compact
   revocation information.  [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert] defines
   C509 certificates, a CBOR encoding of X.509 certificates [RFC5280]
   that reduces certificate size by over 50% for typical constrained-
   device profiles.  However, revocation mechanisms such as Certificate
   Revocation Lists (CRLs) and Online Certificate Status Protocol (OCSP)
   messages have not yet received the same treatment.

   CRLs as defined in [RFC5280] can be large, especially when many
   certificates have been revoked or when CRL extensions add overhead.
   In the complementary OCSP mechanism defined in [RFC6960], OCSP
   responses carry per-certificate status information and may be
   exchanged during TLS [RFC9846] and DTLS [RFC9147] handshakes via OCSP
   stapling [RFC6066], directly affecting handshake latency.

   Note on terminology: The label "C509" is used for consistency with
   the C509 certificate encoding and to promote a cohesive C509 PKI
   ecosystem (C509 certificates, CRLs, OCSP, and related tooling).
   Although this document uses "C509" in its names, the encodings and
   protocols defined here are certificate-type agnostic and apply
   equally to X.509 certificates and other certificate types; see
   Section 7.9.

   This document specifies:

   *  *C509 CRL* -- a natively signed CBOR [RFC8949] encoding of X.509
      CRLs ([RFC5280], Section 5).  The signature is computed over the
      CBOR Sequence [RFC8742] TBSCertList; no ASN.1 encoding is
      involved.  Three key improvements are made over [RFC5280]:

      -  Reduce the search complexity for a given certificate serial
         number from O(n) to O(log(n)):

         o  Replace the implicit certificateIssuer mechanism with
            explicit per-issuer grouping.

         o  Use a dedicated list for the removeFromCRL revocation
            entries.

         o  Use fixed-length entry encoding.

      -  Define a new standalone C509CRLInfo structure to carry CRL
         information without the (large) revocation list itself.

      -  Define dedicated fields for the common extensions to simplify
         the encoding: crlNumber, authorityKeyIdentifier,
         deltaCRLIndicator.

   *  *C509 OCSP* -- a CBOR encoding of OCSP requests and responses
      ([RFC6960]).  The encoding compresses common fields while
      preserving semantic equivalence.  Three key improvements are made
      over [RFC6960]:

      -  The signature in signed requests and responses is computed over
         a wider set of fields than in [RFC6960], including the
         signature algorithm and the certificate chain, preventing
         algorithm-substitution and certificate-chain substitution
         attacks.

      -  The issuer is identified by a single hash over the issuer
         certificate (issuerCertHash) rather than by the two-hash
         CertID, reducing per-issuer overhead.

      -  All participants (requestor, responder, issuer) are identified
         by a hash over their certificate (requestorCertHash,
         responderCertHash, issuerCertHash) rather than by a plaintext
         Subject distinguished name or SubjectKeyIdentifier, avoiding
         structural CHOICE types such as the X.509 ResponderID (byName
         or byKey) and simplifying support for different certificate
         types (e.g., C509, X.509, or future types) because the same
         hash applies regardless of the certificate encoding.

   The CRL and OCSP structures defined in this document are certificate-
   type agnostic: they can be used with C509 certificates, X.509
   certificates, or any future certificate type without modification;
   see Section 7.9.

   C509 CRL and C509 OCSP apply the same compression techniques as C509
   certificates: static fields are elided, OIDs are replaced with short
   integers, time values are compressed to POSIX timestamps, and
   redundant encoding is removed.  Implementers may use the CBOR
   playground [CborMe] to inspect encoded examples.

   C509 CRL and C509 OCSP are not wire-format-compatible with their DER-
   encoded counterparts defined in [RFC5280] and [RFC6960].  Systems
   that use C509 CRL or C509 OCSP must explicitly support these CBOR-
   encoded formats; they cannot be transparently substituted for or
   derived from the corresponding DER-encoded structures.

   C509 CRL and C509 OCSP are designed to be compatible with certificate
   profiles for constrained deployments, such as [RFC7925].

2.  Alternative Certificate Status Checking Mechanisms

   This section considers both widely deployed certificate-status
   mechanisms and closely related prior work for constrained
   environments.  Several such mechanisms can be used to check
   certificate status, but none of them replaces the work in this
   document.

   *  *X.509 CRLs and OCSP* ([RFC5280], [RFC6960]) provide the baseline
      revocation mechanisms, but their DER encodings are not compact
      enough for constrained deployments and are not certificate-type
      agnostic.  This document keeps their semantics while defining CBOR
      encodings that are smaller and easier to process.

   *  *OCSP stapling* ([RFC6066], [RFC9846]) reduces client latency by
      having the server provide a fresh OCSP response, but it does not
      define a revocation status format of its own.  It still depends on
      the underlying OCSP message structure, so it cannot replace a
      compact, standalone status representation.

   *  *Short-lived certificates* reduce reliance on revocation, but they
      do not provide revocation status when certificates must remain
      valid for longer periods or must be withdrawn immediately after
      compromise.  They also shift the problem to more frequent issuance
      and renewal rather than eliminating status checking.

   *  *CRLSets* [CRLSets] are a browser-distributed revocation blocklist
      rather than a general-purpose revocation protocol.  They cover
      only a limited subset of certificates and are tied to a specific
      browser ecosystem, so they cannot provide complete, interoperable
      status checking for arbitrary C509, X.509, or future certificate
      types.

   *  *CRLite* [CRLite] compresses revocation information into a
      browser-oriented filter-based representation, which is useful for
      client-side deployment at scale.  However, it is not a wire-format
      replacement for CRLs or OCSP, does not preserve the full per-
      certificate status structures defined here, and is not a
      certificate-type-agnostic status format that other implementations
      can exchange directly.

   *  *Compact CBOR revocation for IoT (TinyOCSP and CCRL)* [TinyOCSP]
      are prior research mechanisms that reduce OCSP and CRL overhead
      for low-power IoT, confirming the feasibility and benefits of
      CBOR-based revocation on constrained hardware.  They are a
      precursor to this work.  However, they were specified as point
      solutions rather than as interoperable, certificate-type-agnostic
      wire formats aligned with the C509 certificate encoding
      [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert]; this document generalizes those
      mechanisms into complete C509 CRL and C509 OCSP structures usable
      with C509, X.509, and future certificate types.

   The compactness benefit over the DER-encoded baselines is concrete
   and substantial.  For the representative profiles in the worked
   examples (Appendix A), C509 CRLs are 17% to 41% smaller than the
   equivalent X.509 CRLs (for example, a CRL carrying revoked
   certificates is reduced from 421 to 263 bytes, a 38% reduction), and
   C509 OCSP messages are 40% to 69% smaller (for example, a Simple OCSP
   request from 146 to 59 bytes, and a Basic OCSP response without the
   responder's certificate from 821 to 268 bytes); see Table 14.

   These alternatives are useful in specific deployments, but they do
   not provide the CBOR-based, certificate-type-agnostic revocation
   structures defined here for C509, X.509, and future certificate
   types.

4.  Common Types

   The following types are imported from
   [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert]: Name, Extension, Extensions,
   Defined, COSE_C509, and AlgorithmIdentifier.  Additionally, the
   following type is imported from [RFC9360]: COSE_X509.

   This document further defines the following constrained aliases for
   use in all CDDL definitions:

   uint8 = uint .le 255

   uint32 = 0..4294967295             ; 2^32 - 1

   uint63 = 0..9223372036854775807    ; 2^63 - 1

   int16 = -32768..32767

   IntAlgorithmIdentifier = int16

   IntExtension = ( extensionID: IntExtensionID,
                    extensionValue: Defined )

   IntExtensionID = 1..9223372036854775807 / ;   2^63 - 1
                    -9223372036854775807..-1  ; -(2^63 - 1)

   IntExtensions = [ * IntExtension ]

   The CDDL definitions in this document are split into three files:
   c509common.cddl (the definitions above), c509crl.cddl (Section 6),
   and c509ocsp.cddl (Section 7).  Both c509crl.cddl and c509ocsp.cddl
   depend on c509common.cddl and on the types imported from
   [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert] and [RFC9360].

   IntAlgorithmIdentifier is the int-only instantiation of
   AlgorithmIdentifier from [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert].  It is
   used throughout this document wherever an algorithm identifier
   appears in the wire encoding.

   IntExtension is the instantiation of Extension from
   [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert] with an integer extensionID.  As in
   C509 certificates ([I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert],
   Section 3.1.10), the absolute value of extensionID is the value from
   the "C509 Extensions" registry, and its sign indicates whether the
   extension is critical: critical extensions are encoded with a
   negative sign and non-critical extensions with a positive sign.  It
   is used throughout this document wherever a single extension appears
   in the wire encoding.

   IntExtensions is the instantiation of Extensions from
   [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert] where all embedded Extension
   entries are IntExtension entries.  An IntExtensions field with no
   extensions is encoded as an empty array.

5.  Encoding of Time Fields

   Time fields are encoded as unwrapped CBOR epoch-based date/time
   (~time).  The value MUST be a non-negative integer.  In POSIX time
   [POSIX], leap seconds are ignored; a leap second has the same POSIX
   time as the second before it.

6.  C509 CRL

6.1.  Overview

   A C509 CRL is a natively signed CBOR [RFC8949] encoding of an X.509
   v2 CRL as defined in [RFC5280], Section 5.  The signature is computed
   over the CBOR Sequence [RFC8742] TBSCertList; no ASN.1 encoding is
   involved.  A C509 CRL preserves the semantics of the corresponding
   DER-encoded X.509 CRL for the subset of RFC 5280 structures supported
   by this document.

   A C509 CRL is not wire-format-compatible with a DER-encoded X.509
   CRL.  The two formats cannot be mechanically converted to or from
   each other without semantic interpretation of the contained data.

6.2.  C509 CRL CDDL

   The following CDDL defines the top-level C509CertificateList
   structure and the TBSCertList sequence:

   C509CertificateList = [
     TBSCertList,
     signatureValue : any,
   ]

   TBSCertList = (
     CRLInfoData,
     revokedCertsList       : [+ PerIssuerRevokedCerts] / null
   )

   C509CRLInfo = [ CRLInfoData ]

   CRLInfoData = (
     crlType                : 0, ; int16
     signatureAlgorithm     : IntAlgorithmIdentifier,
     authoritySubject       : NameVariant,
     authorityKeyIdentifier : bytes,
     crlNumber              : CRLNumber,
     thisUpdate             : ~time,
     nextUpdate             : uint63 / null,
     baseCrlNumber          : CRLNumber / null,
     crlExtensions          : IntExtensions,
   )

   CRLNumber = 1..9223372036854775807

   PerIssuerRevokedCerts = (
     issuer                 : NameVariant / null,
     revokedCertsControl    : RevokedCertsControl / null,
     extensions             : IntExtensions,
     revokedCerts           : bytes .size (1..2147483647) / null,
     removedFromCRLCerts    : bytes .size (1..2147483647) / null,
   )

   NameVariant = [
     type                   : uint .le 255,
     name                   : any,
   ]

   RevokedCertsControl = [
     flags               : uint32,
     serialNumberLength  : 1..20,
     dateLength          : uint .le 8,
     baseDate            : ~time,
   ]

                   Figure 1: CDDL for C509CertificateList

6.3.  Field Encodings

6.3.1.  CRLInfoData and C509CRLInfo

   CRLInfoData is a CDDL group that carries all CRL fields except
   revokedCertsList.  C509CRLInfo is the standalone CBOR array form of
   CRLInfoData; it can be used to convey CRL information (crlType,
   signature algorithm, issuer identity, CRL number, validity window,
   base CRL number, extensions) without the potentially large revocation
   list.  TBSCertList embeds CRLInfoData and adds revokedCertsList.

6.3.2.  crlType

   The crlType field identifies the format of C509CertificateList and
   C509CRLInfo:

          +=======+=======+=====================================+
          | Value | Type  | Description                         |
          +=======+=======+=====================================+
          |   0   | Basic | The format defined in this document |
          +-------+-------+-------------------------------------+

                             Table 1: CRL types

   Future specifications may define additional types by assigning new
   integer values.

6.3.3.  signatureAlgorithm

   Encoded identically to the int choice of issuerSignatureAlgorithm in
   C509 certificates (see [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert],
   Section 3.1.3).  Algorithm integer values are from the same registry
   used for C509 certificates.

   Unlike in [RFC5280], where the signature algorithm appears outside
   the TBSCertList and is therefore not covered by the CRL signature,
   signatureAlgorithm is part of CRLInfoData within TBSCertList and is
   therefore covered by the signature.  This prevents algorithm-
   substitution attacks on the CRL.

6.3.4.  NameVariant

   A NameVariant carries the name of a CA, together with the type of the
   certificate from which the name is taken.  This allows a C509 CRL to
   identify CAs that hold C509 certificates, X.509 certificates, or
   certificates of other types.

   *  The type field identifies the certificate type.  Its value is
      taken from the "TLS Certificate Types" registry
      [IANA.tls-extensiontype-values].

   *  The name field contains the name, encoded as determined by type.

   This document defines the encoding of name for the following
   certificate types:

   *  type = 0 (X509): name is a byte string containing the DER-encoded
      ([X.690]) X.501 Name ([RFC5280], Section 4.1.2.4).

   *  type = 4 (C509): name is a Name as defined in
      [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert].

   For any other certificate type that defines names, the name is
   encoded as follows, unless the specification of that certificate type
   defines otherwise: if the certificate type is CBOR-encoded, name is
   the CBOR data item that represents the name in the certificate;
   otherwise, name is a byte string containing the name in the original
   encoding of the certificate type.

   A receiver that does not support the certificate type indicated by
   type cannot interpret name.  It MUST NOT match such a NameVariant
   with any certificate.

6.3.5.  authoritySubject and authorityKeyIdentifier

   The authoritySubject and authorityKeyIdentifier fields identify the
   CRL authority.

   The authoritySubject field identifies the subject of the CRL issuer
   as a NameVariant (Section 6.3.4).  Its type is the type of the CRL
   issuer's certificate, and its name is the subject field of that
   certificate.  For example, when the CRL is issued by a C509 CA,
   authoritySubject is [4, subject], where subject is identical to the
   subject field of the authority C509 certificate.  When the CRL is
   issued by an X.509 CA (e.g., in a hybrid deployment where a legacy CA
   issues a C509-format CRL), authoritySubject is [0, subject], where
   subject is a byte string containing the DER-encoded subject field of
   the issuing X.509 CA certificate.

   The authorityKeyIdentifier field is identical to the value of the
   authority's subjectKeyIdentifier extension.  This field corresponds
   to the X.509 CRL extension authorityKeyIdentifier ([RFC5280],
   Section 5.2.1).  It is promoted to a dedicated field because it MUST
   be present in conforming X.509 CRLs and because making it explicit
   simplifies the structure and reduces encoded size compared to
   representing it as a generic extension.

6.3.6.  crlNumber

   The crlNumber field is encoded as a positive integer (CRLNumber, from
   1 to 2^63 - 1).  It corresponds to the cRLNumber extension in
   [RFC5280], Section 5.2.3, which this document replaces with a
   dedicated field.  The value is monotonically increasing for both full
   and delta CRLs.

6.3.7.  thisUpdate and nextUpdate

   The thisUpdate field is encoded as described in Section 5.  The
   nextUpdate field is encoded as a uint63 representing the number of
   seconds from thisUpdate until the next CRL is expected to be issued.
   It is encoded as null when no next update time is known.

6.3.8.  baseCrlNumber

   The baseCrlNumber field is encoded as null for a full CRL.  For a
   delta CRL, it contains the CRL number of the corresponding base full
   CRL.  It corresponds to the deltaCRLIndicator extension in [RFC5280],
   Section 5.2.4, which this document replaces with a dedicated field.

6.3.10.  revokedCertsList and PerIssuerRevokedCerts

6.3.10.1.  Design Considerations

   In X.509 CRLs ([RFC5280], Section 5.3.3), an indirect CRL uses the
   certificateIssuer CRL entry extension to indicate that a revocation
   entry -- and all subsequent entries -- belongs to a different issuer.
   This mechanism has two significant drawbacks:

   *  It is implicit and positional, making it difficult to parse
      correctly and error-prone to implement.

   *  It is impossible to represent an issuer that has no currently
      revoked certificates (e.g., to carry per-issuer extensions or to
      explicitly indicate that the issuer is known but has an empty
      revocation list), because there is no entry to attach the
      certificateIssuer extension to.

   PerIssuerRevokedCerts solves both problems by introducing an explicit
   intermediate structure that groups all revocation information for one
   issuer.  The issuer is identified directly in the issuer field of the
   structure rather than being inferred from the position of a CRL entry
   extension.  This makes the grouping unambiguous and easy to parse,
   and it allows an issuer to appear in the list even when it has no
   revoked certificates.

   A second design problem in X.509 CRLs is that each revocation entry
   has variable length because:

   *  the serial number length differs between certificates,

   *  the revocation date uses different encodings depending on whether
      the date falls before or after 2050,

   *  optional per-entry extensions (e.g., reason code) may or may not
      be present.

   This variable-length layout makes it impossible to determine whether
   a given serial number is present in the list without scanning every
   entry sequentially, resulting in O(n) lookup time.

   In this document, the revokedCertsControl structure introduces fixed
   parameters (serialNumberLength, dateLength) that make every entry
   within revokedCerts exactly the same size.  The flags field can
   additionally indicate that entries are sorted in ascending order by
   serial number, enabling binary search and reducing lookup time to
   O(log(n)).  Using a compact byte-string concatenation rather than a
   CBOR array of structured items further reduces encoded size and
   accelerates parsing.  Furthermore, certificates removed from the CRL
   (delta CRL removeFromCRL entries in X.509) are kept in a dedicated
   removedFromCRLCerts field rather than mixed into revokedCerts.  This
   keeps revokedCerts free of removal noise, reducing its size and
   allowing implementations to process currently revoked and removed
   entries independently.

6.3.10.2.  Fields

   The revokedCertsList field is either a CBOR array of one or more
   PerIssuerRevokedCerts entries, each describing the revocation state
   for certificates issued by one issuer, or CBOR null.  Since
   PerIssuerRevokedCerts is a CDDL group, the fields of each entry are
   inlined in the array rather than wrapped in a nested array; an array
   with n entries therefore contains 5n elements.  revokedCertsList MUST
   be encoded as CBOR null when all of the following conditions hold: no
   certificates are currently revoked, no certificates have been removed
   from the CRL, the only issuer that would appear is the CRL's own
   authoritySubject (i.e., the issuer field is null), and there are no
   non-empty per-issuer extensions.

   Since each PerIssuerRevokedCerts entry identifies its issuer by a
   NameVariant (Section 6.3.4), a single C509 CRL may contain entries
   for certificates of different types.  For example, a CRL may contain
   one PerIssuerRevokedCerts entry for C509 certificates and one for
   X.509 certificates.  All certificates covered by one
   PerIssuerRevokedCerts entry are of the type indicated by the type of
   its issuer, or of authoritySubject if issuer is null; certificates of
   different types are listed in separate entries.

   Each PerIssuerRevokedCerts entry describes revoked certificates for
   one issuer using the following fields:

   *  The issuer field identifies the issuer of the certificates covered
      by this PerIssuerRevokedCerts entry as a NameVariant
      (Section 6.3.4).  Its type is the type of the covered
      certificates, and its name is their issuer field.  For example,
      when the covered certificates are C509 certificates, issuer is [4,
      name], where name is identical to the issuer field of the covered
      C509 certificates; when they are X.509 certificates, issuer is [0,
      name], where name is a byte string containing the DER-encoded
      issuer field of the covered X.509 certificates.  If the issuer is
      identical to the CRL's authoritySubject field, it MUST be encoded
      as CBOR null.

   *  The revokedCertsControl field defines the controls for
      interpreting removedFromCRLCerts and revokedCerts.  If either
      removedFromCRLCerts or revokedCerts is non-null,
      revokedCertsControl MUST NOT be null.  If both removedFromCRLCerts
      and revokedCerts are null, revokedCertsControl MUST be null to
      reduce CRL size.

      -  The flags field is an unsigned integer encoding boolean control
         bits for crlType = 0: bit 0 indicates whether entries in
         revokedCerts are sorted in ascending order by the integer value
         of the certificate serial number; bit 1 indicates whether each
         entry contains a reason code.  Bits 2 and higher are reserved
         for crlType = 0 and MUST be zero and MUST be ignored by
         receivers.  Future crlType values defined in successor
         documents may assign additional bit meanings.

      -  The serialNumberLength field defines the length, in bytes, of a
         certificate serial number.  It MUST be greater than 0.  It is
         the smallest value that can encode all certificate serial
         numbers without leading zeros.

         Each certificate serial number is encoded as a fixed-length
         big-endian integer of length serialNumberLength.  If the
         encoded value is shorter than serialNumberLength, it is left-
         padded with zeros.

      -  The dateLength field defines the length, in bytes, of the big-
         endian encoded revocation-date offset relative to baseDate.  A
         value of 0 means that no revocation date is included in
         revokedCerts entries.  This is permitted in C509-native
         deployments where the revocation date is not required -- for
         example, when minimizing revocation data is more important than
         recording the revocation time.  Implementations that need to
         convert a C509 CRL to an RFC 5280 DER-encoded CRL MUST set
         dateLength to a non-zero value, since [RFC5280], Section 5.1
         requires a revocationDate in every CRL entry.  When dateLength
         is non-zero, the offset MUST fit within the specified byte
         length without leading zero bytes.

      -  The baseDate field is a reference time used to compute
         revocation and removal dates, encoded as described in
         Section 5.  It MUST be greater than or equal to the issuer
         certificate's notBefore, which ensures that all stored offsets
         are non-negative.  It is RECOMMENDED to set baseDate to the
         earliest revocation date and removal-from-CRL date among all
         entries in this CRL, as this minimizes the magnitude of stored
         offsets and reduces encoding size.  Any time between the issuer
         certificate's notBefore (inclusive) and that earliest date
         (inclusive) is a conformant value.  Revocation dates in
         revokedCerts and removal dates in removedFromCRLCerts are
         stored as non-negative offsets, in seconds, from baseDate.

   *  The extensions field contains per-issuer extensions.

   *  The revokedCerts field is a CBOR byte string containing the
      concatenated byte sequence of revoked-certificate entries.  Each
      entry is the concatenation of:

      -  a big-endian encoded certificate serial number of length
         serialNumberLength;

      -  a big-endian encoded revocation-date offset from baseDate, in
         seconds, of length dateLength (absent when dateLength is 0);
         and

      -  an optional reason code (1 byte if bit 1 of flags is set,
         absent otherwise).

      If there are no entries, this field MUST be encoded as CBOR null
      instead of a zero-length byte string.

      The revocation date for a given entry is recovered by adding the
      stored offset to baseDate.  When dateLength is 0, no revocation
      date is stored and no date recovery is possible.

      Revoked-certificate entries have a fixed length.  If bit 0 of
      flags is set, entries are sorted in ascending order, enabling
      binary search for a given serial number in O(log(n)) time.

   *  The removedFromCRLCerts field contains a concatenated byte
      sequence of entries for certificates removed from the CRL since
      publication of the CRL with crlNumber = baseCrlNumber.  Each entry
      is the concatenation of:

      -  a big-endian encoded certificate serial number of length
         serialNumberLength; and

      -  a big-endian encoded removal-date offset from baseDate, in
         seconds, of length dateLength (absent when dateLength is 0).

      If there are no entries, this field MUST be encoded as CBOR null
      instead of a zero-length byte string.

      The removal date for a given entry is recovered by adding the
      stored offset to baseDate.  When dateLength is 0, no removal date
      is stored and no date recovery is possible.

      Removed-certificate entries have a fixed length.  If bit 0 of
      flags is set, entries are sorted in ascending order, enabling
      binary search for a given serial number in O(log(n)) time.

7.  C509 OCSP

7.1.  Overview

   C509 OCSP defines CBOR encodings for OCSP requests and OCSP responses
   as defined in [RFC6960].  The encoding follows the same compression
   principles as C509 CRL and C509 certificates.

   C509 OCSP requests and responses are not wire-format-compatible with
   DER-encoded OCSP messages as defined in [RFC6960].  The two formats
   cannot be mechanically converted to or from each other without
   semantic interpretation of the contained data.

   C509OCSPRequest and C509OCSPResponse are designed to be extensible.
   The first field of every message is ocspRequestType or
   ocspResponseType, an integer that identifies the structure type.
   Future specifications may define additional types by assigning new
   integer values, enabling new request or response structures to be
   introduced without modifying the existing types.

7.2.  Conformance Requirements

   To ensure interoperability, implementations of C509 OCSP MUST support
   the following message types:

   *  An OCSP client MUST support C509SimpleOCSPRequest
      (Section 7.7.2.5) and C509SimpleOCSPResponse (Section 7.8.2.3).
      An OCSP client MAY support C509BasicOCSPRequest.  If an OCSP
      client supports C509BasicOCSPRequest, it MUST support
      C509BasicOCSPResponse.

   *  An OCSP responder MUST support C509SimpleOCSPRequest,
      C509SimpleOCSPResponse, C509BasicOCSPRequest, and
      C509BasicOCSPResponse.

   *  Both OCSP clients and OCSP responders MUST support
      C509ErrorOCSPResponse.

7.3.  Hash Algorithms

   The hashAlgorithm field of C509 OCSP requests and responses
   identifies the hash algorithm used to compute the certificate hashes
   (issuerCertHash, requestorCertHash, and responderCertHash) within the
   enclosing structure.  Its value is the integer identifier of a hash
   algorithm from the "COSE Algorithms" registry [IANA.cose], e.g., -16
   for SHA-256, -43 for SHA-384, and -44 for SHA-512 [RFC9054].  Any
   hash algorithm in that registry can be used, subject to the
   restrictions below; for example, SHA3-256 or SM3 can be used once
   they are registered.

   The hash algorithm MUST produce an output whose length is at least
   that of the HashedId values derived from it (see Section 7.4).  SHA-
   256/64 (-15) MUST NOT be used.  Other truncated hash algorithms, such
   as SHA-512/256 (-17), SHOULD NOT be used: this document already
   defines the truncated identifier HashedId, so using a truncated hash
   algorithm provides no benefit.  SHA-1 (-14) MUST NOT be used either,
   since it is not collision resistant.  For SHAKE128 (-18) and SHAKE256
   (-45), the output length is as specified in [RFC9054], i.e., 256 and
   512 bits, respectively.  For an extendable-output function whose
   output length is not fixed by its registration, such as TurboSHAKE128
   (-261) and TurboSHAKE256 (-262) [RFC9861], the output length is the
   length of the HashedId being computed.

7.4.  Identification of Certificates

   To reduce OCSP message size, this document defines a truncated-hash
   identifier type used to refer to certificates compactly:

   *  HashedId: the leading bytes, i.e., the leftmost bytes, of the hash
      value computed using the enclosing hashAlgorithm.  A HashedId is 8
      to 64 bytes long.  This is aligned with the truncation of hash
      values in SHA-512/256 [FIPS180-4] and SHA-256/64 [RFC9054], which
      also keep the leftmost bits.  HashedId is used to identify issuer
      certificates and the certificates of OCSP requestors and
      responders in request and response structures.

   HashedId = bytes .size (8..64)

   The sender chooses the length of a HashedId; it MUST NOT be longer
   than the output of the hash algorithm.  The length is a trade-off
   between message size and collision resistance; see Section 9.2.  A
   receiver compares a received HashedId with a certificate by computing
   the hash value of the certificate using hashAlgorithm and comparing
   its leading bytes, as many as the received HashedId has, with the
   received HashedId.  An OCSP responder MUST use in a response the same
   issuerCertHash value as in the corresponding request.

7.5.  Certificate Chains

   The requestorCerts and responderCerts fields carry the ordered
   certificate chain of the signer as a CertChainVariant, together with
   the type of the certificates in the chain.  This follows the same
   pattern as NameVariant (Section 6.3.4).

   CertChainVariant = [
     type  : uint .le 255,
     chain : any,
   ]

   *  The type field identifies the certificate type.  Its value is
      taken from the "TLS Certificate Types" registry
      [IANA.tls-extensiontype-values].

   *  The chain field contains the ordered certificate chain, starting
      with the signer's certificate, encoded as determined by type.

   This document defines the encoding of chain for the following
   certificate types:

   *  type = 0 (X509): chain is a COSE_X509 ([RFC9360]) containing an
      ordered chain of X.509 certificates.

   *  type = 4 (C509): chain is a COSE_C509
      ([I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert]) containing an ordered chain of
      C509 certificates.

   For any other certificate type, the ordered certificate chain is
   encoded as follows, unless the specification of that certificate type
   defines otherwise: if the certificate type is CBOR-encoded, chain is
   a CBOR array of the CBOR data items representing the certificates;
   otherwise, chain is a CBOR array of byte strings, each containing a
   certificate in the original encoding of the certificate type.

   A receiver that does not support the certificate type indicated by
   type cannot interpret chain.  It MUST NOT use such a CertChainVariant
   to verify the signature.

7.6.  Certificate Serial Numbers

   The certificate whose status is requested or reported is identified
   by issuerCertHash together with its serial number, carried in the
   serialNumber field.

   SerialNumber = bytes .size (1..20)

   The serialNumber field contains the serial number of the target
   certificate, encoded as a byte string.  Its value depends on the
   certificate type.

   For a C509 certificate, serialNumber is the certificateSerialNumber
   of the certificate ([I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert]) as is.

   For an X.509 certificate, the serial number is an ASN.1 INTEGER
   ([RFC5280], Section 4.1.2.2).  Its DER encoding is two's complement
   and therefore contains a leading zero byte when the most-significant
   bit of the value is set.  This leading zero byte is not part of
   serialNumber: the value is the contents octets of the DER-encoded
   INTEGER with a leading zero byte removed (if present).  For example,
   the DER-encoded serial number 02 03 00 8F 01 gives serialNumber
   h'8F01', and the DER-encoded serial number 02 02 0F 01 gives
   serialNumber h'0F01'.

   [RFC5280], Section 4.1.2.2 requires serial numbers to be positive.  A
   non-conforming X.509 certificate with serial number zero (DER-encoded
   02 01 00) would result in an empty serialNumber, which is not a valid
   SerialNumber.  An OCSP responder MUST reject a request with an
   invalid serialNumber by returning a C509ErrorOCSPResponse with
   responseStatus set to malformedRequest (1).

   As in the CertID of [RFC6960], the serial number is carried in plain
   form and is not hashed.  Serial numbers are not secret, and an
   unsalted hash would not prevent an observer with a set of candidate
   serial numbers from identifying the queried certificate (see
   Section 9.3).  The plain serial number is at most 20 bytes long, lets
   an OCSP responder look up the certificate directly, and cannot
   collide.

7.7.2.2.  issuerCertHash

   The issuerCertHash field appears in PerIssuerOCSPRequest,
   PerIssuerOCSPResponse, C509SimpleOCSPRequest, and
   TBSSimpleOCSPResponse.  It contains a hash of the issuer certificate:

   *  issuerCertHash: hash computed over the deterministically encoded
      certificate, i.e., the CBOR-encoded C509Certificate or the DER-
      encoded ([X.690]) X.509 certificate.  For a CBOR re-encoded X.509
      certificate (c509CertificateType = 3, see
      [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert], Section 3.1.1), the hash MUST
      be computed over the DER-encoded X.509 certificate, not over its
      C509 encoding, since such a certificate may have more than one
      conformant CBOR encoding (see [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert],
      Section 3.4).  The hash algorithm is specified by the
      hashAlgorithm field of the enclosing request or response
      structure.

   In X.509 OCSP ([RFC6960], Section 4.1.1), the CertID structure
   identifies the issuer using two separate hashes: issuerNameHash (a
   hash of the DER-encoded issuer name) and issuerKeyHash (a hash of the
   issuer's public key bit string).  This two-hash approach was designed
   for interoperability with DER-encoded X.509 structures, but it
   doubles the per-issuer overhead in the message.  In C509 OCSP, the
   issuer is identified by a single hash of the issuer certificate
   (issuerCertHash), which is simpler, smaller, and unambiguous.
   Implementations that need to cross-reference a C509 issuerCertHash
   with an RFC 6960 DER-encoded CertID must recompute the respective
   hash inputs; the values are not directly comparable.

7.7.2.3.  PerIssuerOCSPRequests, PerIssuerOCSPRequest,
          SingleCertRequests and SingleCertRequest

   PerIssuerOCSPRequests is a list of PerIssuerOCSPRequest entries.  In
   X.509 OCSP ([RFC6960], Section 4.1.1), every Request entry embeds a
   full CertID containing the issuer identity fields (hashAlgorithm,
   issuerNameHash, issuerKeyHash) alongside the serialNumber.  When
   multiple certificates from the same issuer are queried in a single
   request, these issuer fields are repeated verbatim for every entry,
   wasting space.  PerIssuerOCSPRequest solves this by grouping all
   single-certificate requests for the same issuer under one entry: the
   issuerCertHash field is encoded once and shared across all
   SingleCertRequest entries in the singleRequests list, eliminating the
   per-entry duplication.  Since PerIssuerOCSPRequest and
   SingleCertRequest are CDDL groups, the fields of each entry are
   inlined in the enclosing array rather than wrapped in a nested array.

   *  The extensions field contains per-issuer extensions that apply to
      all single-certificate requests in this PerIssuerOCSPRequest
      entry; see Section 7.8.2.12.

   *  The singleRequests field is a list of SingleCertRequest entries
      for certificates from this issuer.

   Each SingleCertRequest contains:

   *  The serialNumber field is the certificate serial number of the
      target certificate; see Section 7.6.

   *  The extensions field contains extensions for the single request;
      see Section 7.8.2.13.

7.7.2.4.  C509BasicOCSPRequest

   C509BasicOCSPRequest is used for both unsigned and signed requests.
   A request is signed if and only if signatureAlgorithm is not null.
   An unsigned request ends with the requests field; a signed request
   additionally contains the fields requestorCertHash, requestorCerts,
   and signatureValue.  An OCSP responder MUST reject a request in which
   signatureAlgorithm is null and these fields are present, or in which
   signatureAlgorithm is not null and these fields are absent, by
   returning a C509ErrorOCSPResponse with responseStatus set to
   malformedRequest (1).

   *  The ocspRequestType field MUST be 1.

   *  The signatureAlgorithm field specifies the signature algorithm, as
      defined in [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert], Section 3.1.3, or
      MUST be null if the request is unsigned.  It is placed before the
      other fields, and not in the group of fields present only in a
      signed request, so that a receiver knows the signature algorithm
      before processing the signed data, which allows one-pass signature
      verification.

   *  The hashAlgorithm field specifies the hash algorithm used to
      compute issuerCertHash and, in a signed request,
      requestorCertHash.  The algorithm is encoded as specified in
      Section 7.3.

   *  The nonce field contains the request nonce, or MUST be null when
      no nonce is present.  The nonce MUST be generated by a
      cryptographically strong pseudorandom number generator and MUST be
      between 16 and 128 bytes long.  An OCSP responder MUST reject a
      request whose nonce is shorter than 16 bytes or longer than 128
      bytes by returning a C509ErrorOCSPResponse with responseStatus set
      to malformedRequest (1).

   *  The extensions field contains request-level extensions; see
      Section 7.8.2.13.

   *  The requests field is a list of PerIssuerOCSPRequest entries, each
      grouping target certificates by issuer; see Section 7.7.2.3.

   *  The requestorCertHash field, present only in a signed request, is
      a hash of the signer's certificate, computed using hashAlgorithm.

   *  The requestorCerts field, present only in a signed request,
      contains the ordered certificate chain of the signer as a
      CertChainVariant (Section 7.5), so that the receiver can verify
      the identity of the requestor, or MUST be null if no certificates
      are transported.

   *  The signatureValue field, present only in a signed request, is the
      signature computed over the CBOR Sequence of all elements of the
      C509BasicOCSPRequest array except signatureValue, i.e., from
      ocspRequestType to requestorCerts.

7.7.2.5.  C509SimpleOCSPRequest

   C509SimpleOCSPRequest is a compact request format for querying the
   status of a single certificate.  Unlike C509BasicOCSPRequest, which
   uses a two-level PerIssuerOCSPRequests / SingleCertRequest grouping
   to batch multiple serial numbers across one or more issuers,
   C509SimpleOCSPRequest flattens the structure into a single CBOR array
   for the common case of a one-certificate query.

   *  The ocspRequestType field MUST be 2.

   *  The hashAlgorithm field specifies the hash algorithm used to
      compute issuerCertHash.  The algorithm is encoded as specified in
      Section 7.3.

   *  The nonce field is as defined for C509BasicOCSPRequest
      (Section 7.7.2.4).

   *  The issuerCertHash field identifies the issuer of the target
      certificate; see Section 7.7.2.2.

   *  The serialNumber field is the certificate serial number of the
      target certificate; see Section 7.6.

   *  The extensions field contains request-level extensions; see
      Section 7.8.2.13.

7.8.2.2.  C509BasicOCSPResponse

   The ocspResponseType field of C509BasicOCSPResponse is 1,
   distinguishing it from C509ErrorOCSPResponse and
   C509SimpleOCSPResponse.  It corresponds to BasicOCSPResponse in
   [RFC6960].

   TBSBasicOCSPResponse is the group of fields to be signed.  The
   signatureValue field is the only field outside the signed group.
   Covering signatureAlgorithm and responderCerts within the signed
   content prevents algorithm-substitution and certificate-chain
   substitution attacks; this design goes beyond the protections offered
   by the DER-encoded BasicOCSPResponse defined in [RFC6960].

7.8.2.3.  C509SimpleOCSPResponse

   C509SimpleOCSPResponse is a compact signed response for a single-
   certificate status query.  It is the response counterpart to
   C509SimpleOCSPRequest (see Section 7.7.2.5).  Unlike
   C509BasicOCSPResponse, which uses a two-level PerIssuerOCSPResponses
   / SingleCertResponse grouping to carry status for multiple
   certificates across one or more issuers, C509SimpleOCSPResponse
   flattens the structure into a single signed CBOR array for the common
   case of a one-certificate response.

   TBSSimpleOCSPResponse is the group of fields to be signed.  The
   signatureValue field is the only field outside the signed group.
   Covering signatureAlgorithm, responderCertHash, and responderCerts
   within the signed content prevents algorithm-substitution and
   certificate-chain substitution attacks.

   *  The ocspResponseType field MUST be 2, which discriminates
      C509SimpleOCSPResponse from C509ErrorOCSPResponse and
      C509BasicOCSPResponse.

   *  The signatureAlgorithm field specifies the signature algorithm, as
      defined in [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert], Section 3.1.3.  It
      is part of TBSSimpleOCSPResponse and is therefore covered by the
      signature.

   *  The hashAlgorithm field specifies the hash algorithm used to
      compute responderCertHash and issuerCertHash.  The algorithm is
      encoded as specified in Section 7.3.

   *  The nonce field contains the request nonce, or MUST be null when
      no nonce is present.  If present, it MUST have the same value as
      the nonce in the corresponding C509SimpleOCSPRequest.

   *  The responderCertHash field identifies the OCSP responder; see
      Section 7.8.2.5.

   *  The issuerCertHash field identifies the issuer of the queried
      certificate; see Section 7.7.2.2.

   *  The serialNumber field is the certificate serial number of the
      target certificate; see Section 7.6.

   *  The certStatus field encodes the revocation status; see
      Section 7.8.2.11.

   *  The producedAt field is the time at which this response was
      produced, encoded as described in Section 5.

   *  The thisUpdate field is encoded as a non-positive integer (nint /
      0) representing the number of seconds from producedAt at which the
      indicated status is known to be correct.  It MUST be 0 or
      negative, since the status cannot be known at a time in the future
      relative to the time the response was produced.

   *  The nextUpdate field is encoded as a uint63 representing the
      number of seconds from producedAt until the next response update
      is expected, or null if the update time is unknown.

   *  The extensions field contains response-level extensions; see
      Section 7.8.2.13.

   *  The responderCerts field carries the certificate chain starting
      with the responder certificate identified by responderCertHash;
      see Section 7.8.2.6.

   *  The signatureValue field is the signature computed over the CBOR
      Sequence of the TBSSimpleOCSPResponse group.

7.8.2.4.  nonce

   If present, this field MUST have the same value as the nonce in the
   corresponding request.

7.8.2.5.  responderCertHash

   responderCertHash is a truncated hash over the responder's
   certificate, computed using the same hashAlgorithm as issuerCertHash.
   In X.509 OCSP ([RFC6960]), the responder is identified by a
   ResponderID CHOICE that carries either a distinguished name or a
   public-key hash.  C509 OCSP replaces this with a single hash over the
   entire responder certificate, which is consistent with the
   issuerCertHash design for issuer identification.

7.8.2.6.  responderCerts

   When present, the responderCerts field carries the certificate chain
   starting with the responder certificate identified by
   responderCertHash, as a CertChainVariant (Section 7.5).  The field is
   part of TBSBasicOCSPResponse or TBSSimpleOCSPResponse and is
   therefore covered by the signature, which prevents certificate-chain
   substitution attacks.

7.8.2.7.  signatureAlgorithm and signatureValue

   *  signatureAlgorithm: the signature algorithm used to compute
      signatureValue, as defined in [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert],
      Section 3.1.3.  It is part of the TBSBasicOCSPResponse or
      TBSSimpleOCSPResponse group and is therefore covered by the
      signature.

   *  signatureValue: the signature computed over the CBOR Sequence of
      the TBSBasicOCSPResponse or TBSSimpleOCSPResponse group.

7.8.2.8.  producedAt, thisUpdate, nextUpdate

   The producedAt field is encoded as described in Section 5.  The
   thisUpdate field is encoded as a non-positive integer (nint / 0)
   representing the number of seconds from producedAt at which the
   indicated status is known to be correct; it MUST be 0 or negative,
   since the status cannot be known at a time in the future relative to
   when the response was produced.  The nextUpdate field is encoded as a
   uint63 representing the number of seconds from producedAt until the
   next update is expected to be available.  nextUpdate may be null,
   which indicates an unknown update time.

7.8.2.9.  extensions

   See Section 7.8.2.13.

7.8.2.10.  PerIssuerOCSPResponses and PerIssuerOCSPResponse

   PerIssuerOCSPResponses is a list of PerIssuerOCSPResponse entries.
   For the same reason as on the request side (see Section 7.7.2.3), the
   issuerCertHash field is encoded once per issuer and shared across all
   SingleCertResponse entries in the singleResponses list, avoiding the
   per-entry repetition that would occur if the issuer identity were
   embedded in every individual response entry.  PerIssuerOCSPResponse
   groups single responses for certificates issued by the same issuer;
   the issuer is identified by issuerCertHash (see Section 7.7.2.2).  As
   on the request side, PerIssuerOCSPResponse and SingleCertResponse are
   CDDL groups whose fields are inlined in the enclosing array.

   *  The extensions field contains per-issuer extensions that apply to
      all single-certificate responses in this PerIssuerOCSPResponse
      entry; see Section 7.8.2.12.

   *  The singleResponses field contains one SingleCertResponse for each
      queried certificate issued by that issuer.

7.8.2.11.  SingleCertResponse

7.8.2.11.1.  serialNumber

   This field contains the serial number of the certificate being
   queried; see Section 7.6.

7.8.2.11.2.  extensions

   See Section 7.8.2.13.

7.8.2.11.3.  certStatus

   CertStatus is encoded either as an integer (for good, not-issued, and
   unknown) or as a RevokedInfo array:

   *  0: good -- the certificate is not revoked.

   *  1: not-issued -- the issuer is recognized but the serial number is
      unknown.

   *  2: unknown -- the responder does not recognize the issuer
      identified by issuerCertHash.

   *  RevokedInfo: revoked -- an array carrying revocationTime and
      revocationReason.  The revocationReason field is encoded as
      specified in Section 6.3.9.  When the revocation reason is not
      known, revocationReason MUST be set to 0 (unspecified).

   The integer values are chosen to align with the ASN.1 IMPLICIT tag
   numbers of CertStatus in [RFC6960]: good uses ASN.1 tag 0 and unknown
   uses ASN.1 tag 2.  The ASN.1 revoked alternative uses ASN.1 tag 1;
   since C509 represents the revoked status as the explicit RevokedInfo
   array, the integer value 1 becomes available.  It is assigned to the
   not-issued status, which was introduced in [RFC6960], Section 4.4.8
   to distinguish unrecognized serial numbers from unrecognized issuers,
   thereby maintaining a contiguous integer range for the non-revoked
   statuses.

   the "orig" field of a PASSporT being validated).

7.9.  Certificate Type Interoperability

   C509 OCSP uses a single set of request and response structures that
   handles C509 certificates and X.509 certificates without
   modification.  All participants (requestor, responder, issuer) are
   identified by a hash over their certificate rather than by type-
   specific fields such as a Subject distinguished name,
   SubjectKeyIdentifier, or the X.509 ResponderID CHOICE (byName or
   byKey).  Because a hash applies uniformly regardless of the
   certificate encoding, the same OCSP structures accommodate C509
   certificates (CBOR-encoded), X.509 certificates (DER-encoded), and
   any future certificate types.

   The only per-type differences are in how identity hashes and serial
   numbers are computed and how certificate chains are carried:

        +=====================+=================+=================+
        | Field               | C509            | X.509           |
        |                     | certificate     | certificate     |
        +=====================+=================+=================+
        | issuerCertHash /    | Hash of CBOR-   | Hash of DER-    |
        | requestorCertHash / | encoded         | encoded X.509   |
        | responderCertHash   | C509Certificate | certificate     |
        +---------------------+-----------------+-----------------+
        | serialNumber        | Serial number   | Serial number   |
        |                     | as is           | without leading |
        |                     |                 | zero byte       |
        +---------------------+-----------------+-----------------+
        | requestorCerts /    | [4, COSE_C509]  | [0, COSE_X509]  |
        | responderCerts      |                 |                 |
        +---------------------+-----------------+-----------------+

        Table 10: Per-type differences in C509 OCSP identity fields

   In this table, "C509 certificate" refers to a natively signed C509
   certificate (c509CertificateType = 2).  A CBOR re-encoded X.509
   certificate (c509CertificateType = 3) is treated as an X.509
   certificate: its identity hash MUST be computed over the DER-encoded
   X.509 certificate.

   In a hybrid deployment where the responder holds an X.509
   certificate, responderCertHash contains the hash of the DER-encoded
   X.509 certificate and responderCerts carries [0, COSE_X509].  In a
   deployment using C509 certificates throughout, all identity hashes
   are computed over CBOR-encoded certificates and certificate chains
   are carried as [4, COSE_C509].  The hashAlgorithm field at the top
   level of each request or response structure governs all certificate
   hashes within that structure.

7.10.  OCSP Stapling in TLS

   In standard TLS certificate status stapling (see the status_request
   extension, [RFC9846]), the stapled OCSP response is a DER-encoded
   OCSPResponse.  To allow C509 OCSP stapling, this document defines a
   new TLS CertificateStatusType value (see [RFC6066], Section 8):

                   +=======+===========+===============+

9.  Security Considerations

   The security considerations of [RFC5280], [RFC6960],
   [I-D.ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert], and [RFC8949], Section 10 apply.
   This section covers the considerations specific to this document.

9.1.  Signature Verification

   The signatures of C509 CRLs and of signed C509 OCSP messages cover
   all fields except signatureValue, including signatureAlgorithm and
   requestorCerts or responderCerts.  This prevents algorithm and
   certificate-chain substitution, but does not replace the validation
   of [RFC5280] and [RFC6960]: the verifier MUST check that
   signatureAlgorithm matches the signer's public key, MUST validate the
   signer's certificate chain (a transported chain is only a hint for
   path construction), and MUST verify that the signer is authorized, as
   specified in [RFC6960], Section 4.2.2.2 for OCSP responses and in
   [RFC5280], Section 6.3 for CRLs.  The signature SHOULD be verified
   over the bytes as received rather than over a re-encoding.

   C509ErrorOCSPResponse and C509CRLInfo (Section 6.3.1) are not signed.
   An error response MUST NOT be interpreted as status information about
   any certificate.  A C509CRLInfo MUST NOT be used for revocation or
   freshness decisions unless it was obtained over a channel that
   authenticates the CRL issuer.

9.2.  Truncated Hash Identifiers

   HashedId (Section 7.4) only selects a certificate and does not
   authenticate it; security relies on the signature verification in
   Section 9.1.  A relying party MUST match a response to its request
   using hash values it has computed itself from the certificates it
   holds.  A receiver that finds several certificates matching a
   HashedId MUST NOT assume that a particular one is intended.  An OCSP
   responder for which an issuerCertHash matches more than one of its
   issuers MUST NOT return the status good for that entry.

   HashedId identifies only the certificates of CAs, OCSP requestors,
   and OCSP responders; the certificate whose status is queried is
   identified by its serial number, not by a hash.  The number of
   certificates among which a HashedId must be unique is therefore
   small, typically the CA and responder certificates known to a relying
   party or the CA certificates served by an OCSP responder.  For a
   HashedId of L bytes, the probability that any two of n distinct
   certificates have the same HashedId is about n^2 / 2^(8L+1).  For n =
   10^6 such certificates, this is about 2^-25 for L = 8, about 2^-89
   (below 10^-26) for L = 16, and about 2^-121 (below 10^-36) for L =
   20.

   Finding a certificate whose HashedId equals that of a given
   certificate, e.g., to make a response for one certificate apply to
   another, requires about 2^(8L) operations for a secure hash
   algorithm, i.e., 2^64 for L = 8 and 2^128 for L = 16.  An attacker
   can find two certificates with the same HashedId with about 2^(4L)
   operations, but both certificates are then under the attacker's
   control; since HashedId only selects a certificate and trust is
   established by the signature verification and the authorization
   checks in Section 9.1, such a collision gives the attacker no
   advantage.  Since the serial number is carried in plain form, the
   pair of issuerCertHash and serialNumber is as unambiguous as
   issuerCertHash alone.

   A HashedId of 8 bytes is sufficient in closed deployments with a
   small number of CA, requestor, and responder certificates.  Where an
   attacker may be able to obtain certificates with chosen content from
   a CA that the relying party trusts, a HashedId of at least 16 bytes
   SHOULD be used.

   The serial number MUST be normalized as specified in Section 7.6;
   otherwise, a revoked certificate is not found.

9.3.  Privacy

   As in [RFC6960], the serial number in a request or response is not
   protected: an observer can identify the queried certificate and link
   repeated queries, and issuerCertHash reveals the issuer.  Where this
   matters, requests and responses SHOULD be protected, e.g., by DTLS or
   OSCORE, or OCSP stapling (Section 7.10) SHOULD be used.

9.4.  Freshness and Replay

   A relying party MUST check the validity interval of an OCSP response
   ([RFC6960], Section 4.2.2.1) and, if it sent a nonce, that the
   response carries the same nonce.  Without a nonce, a response can be
   replayed until it expires; relying parties without a reliable clock
   SHOULD use nonces.

   The nonce is 16 to 128 bytes long (Section 7.7.2.4), whereas
   [RFC9654] requires OCSP requesters to use at least 32 octets in the
   Nonce extension of [RFC6960]; the maximum of 128 bytes is the same as
   in [RFC9654].  A replay succeeds only if the attacker has obtained,
   from the OCSP responder, a response for the nonce that the relying
   party later chooses.  With N such responses and M requests, the
   probability is about N * M / 2^128 for a 16-byte nonce, i.e., about
   2^-48 even for N = M = 2^40, and the attack can be neither performed
   offline nor accelerated by a quantum computer.  A 32-byte nonce adds
   no practical security but 16 bytes to each request and response,
   which is significant on constrained links.

   A relying party MUST NOT use a CRL after thisUpdate + nextUpdate,
   SHOULD reject a CRL whose crlNumber is lower than that of a CRL
   previously accepted for the same scope, and MUST combine a delta CRL
   only with a full CRL whose crlNumber is at least baseCrlNumber
   ([RFC5280], Section 5.2.4).

9.5.  Completeness of CRLs

   *  Certificates whose serial number is not a positive integer of at
      most 20 bytes, as required by [RFC5280], Section 4.1.2.2, are not
      supported by this document; C509 CRLs cannot list some of them,
      and relying parties MUST reject them.

   *  When dateLength is 0, no revocation date is available, and a
      listed certificate MUST be treated as revoked at all times.

   *  When bit 1 of flags is not set, no reason code is available, and
      the reason MUST be treated as unspecified.

   *  The issuer MUST be compared in the representation that matches the
      certificate type, as indicated by the type of the NameVariant
      (Section 6.3.4).

   Implementations MUST reject a CRL in which the length of revokedCerts
   or removedFromCRLCerts is not a multiple of the entry length.  An
   implementation that uses binary search based on bit 0 of flags MUST
   verify that the entries are sorted, since otherwise a revoked
   certificate can be missed.

9.6.  Parsing and Denial of Service

   Messages are parsed before their signature is verified, and unsigned
   requests are never authenticated.  Implementations MUST reject input
   that does not conform to the CDDL, SHOULD limit message sizes and the
   number of entries, and MUST reject input for which computing an
   absolute time from a base time and an offset overflows.

   Answering a request may require a signature, and responses are larger
   than requests, which exposes OCSP responders to resource exhaustion
   and to abuse for traffic amplification over UDP ([RFC7252],
   Section 11.3).  OCSP responders SHOULD rate-limit requests and MAY
   require signed requests.

   A stapled C509SimpleOCSPResponse (Section 7.10) MUST be validated as
   if obtained directly from the OCSP responder.

Appendix B.  Document Updates

   This section is to be removed before publishing as an RFC.

B.1.  Version -01 to -02

   *  Replaced serialNumberHash in OCSP requests and responses with the
      plain serial number (serialNumber).

   *  Replaced HashId8 with HashedId, the leading 8 to 64 bytes of the
      hash value, for the certificate hashes issuerCertHash,
      requestorCertHash, and responderCertHash, and discussed the
      collision probability in the Security Considerations.

   *  Merged C509UnsignedOCSPRequest and C509SignedOCSPRequest into
      C509BasicOCSPRequest (type 1).

   *  Renamed C509CRL to C509CertificateList, aligned with
      CertificateList in [RFC5280], and named CRL type 0 "Basic".

   *  Introduced NameVariant for the CRL fields authoritySubject and
      issuer, replacing Name / #6.121(bytes).  The name type is taken
      from the "TLS Certificate Types" registry.

   *  Introduced CertChainVariant for the OCSP fields requestorCerts and
      responderCerts, replacing COSE_C509 / #6.121(COSE_X509).

   *  Added the section "Conformance Requirements" for C509 OCSP clients
      and responders.

   *  Restricted TLS OCSP stapling to C509SimpleOCSPResponse.

B.2.  Version -00 to -01

   *  Added the section "Alternative Certificate Status Checking
      Mechanisms".

   *  Constrained the value ranges of integer fields in the CDDL.

   *  Replaced the "C509 Hash Algorithms" registry with the "COSE
      Algorithms" registry.

   *  Specified the PEM representation of C509 CRL and C509 OCSP
      messages.

   *  Added the registries for C509 CRL types and C509 OCSP request and
      response types.

   *  Added the section "Implementation Status".

   *  Added the section "Security Considerations".

   *  Clarifications and editorial improvements.
